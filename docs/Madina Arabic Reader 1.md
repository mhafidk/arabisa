# Lesson 1 (الدَّرْسُ الأَوَّلُ)

## 1. Core Concept: The Demonstrative Pronoun "This" (هَذَا)
This lesson introduces the masculine, singular demonstrative pronoun **هَذَا** (hâdhâ), which translates to "this". It is used to point to objects or people that are close by.

## 2. Grammar Notes & Points to Remember
*   **Pronunciation of هَذَا:** It is pronounced *hâdhâ* (with a long 'a' after the 'h'), even though it is written without the first *alif*.
*   **The "Is" Copula:** Arabic has no word corresponding exactly to the English "is" in these types of sentences. "This is a house" is simply "This (a) house" (هَذَا بَيْتٌ).
*   **The Indefinite Article ("a" / "an"):** There is no separate word for "a" or "an". The "-n" sound at the end of an Arabic noun (e.g., *baitu-n*, *masjidu-n*) acts as the indefinite article. This "-n" sound is called **tanwīn** (تَنْوِين), represented by the double vowel marker (ٌ  ).
*   **Forming Questions:**
    *   **What is...? (مَا):** Used for objects/animals. Example: مَا هَذَا؟ (What is this?)
    *   **Who is...? (مَنْ):** Used for people/intellects. Example: مَنْ هَذَا؟ (Who is this?)
    *   **Is this...? (أَ):** Placing the particle **أَ** (a) at the beginning of a statement turns it into a Yes/No question. 
        *   Example: أَهَذَا بَيْتٌ؟ (Is this a house?)

## 4. Example Sentences & Dialogues

**Basic Statements:**
*   هَذَا بَيْتٌ. (This is a house.)
*   هَذَا مَسْجِدٌ. (This is a mosque.)
*   هَذَا مِفْتَاحٌ. (This is a key.)

**Asking "What is this?" (مَا هَذَا؟):**
*   **Q:** مَا هَذَا؟ (What is this?)
*   **A:** هَذَا قَمِيصٌ. (This is a shirt.)
*   **Q:** مَا هَذَا؟ (What is this?)
*   **A:** هَذَا نَجْمٌ. (This is a star.)

**Yes/No Questions (أَهَذَا...؟):**
*   **Q:** أَهَذَا بَيْتٌ؟ (Is this a house?)
*   **A:** نَعَمْ، هَذَا بَيْتٌ. (Yes, this is a house.)
*   **Q:** أَهَذَا مِفْتَاحٌ؟ (Is this a key?)
*   **A:** لا، هَذَا قَلَمٌ. (No, this is a pen.)
*   **Q:** أَهَذَا سَرِيرٌ؟ (Is this a bed?)
*   **A:** لا، هَذَا كُرْسِيٌّ. (No, this is a chair.)

**Asking "Who is this?" (مَنْ هَذَا؟):**
*   **Q:** مَنْ هَذَا؟ (Who is this?)
*   **A:** هَذَا طَبِيبٌ. (This is a doctor.)
*   **Q:** مَنْ هَذَا؟ (Who is this?)
*   **A:** هَذَا طَالِبٌ. (This is a student.)
*   **Q:** أَهَذَا وَلَدٌ؟ (Is this a boy?)
*   **A:** لا، هَذَا رَجُلٌ. (No, this is a man.)

# Lesson 2 (الدَّرْسُ الثَّانِي)

## 1. Core Concepts: "That" (ذَلِكَ) and "And" (وَ)
This lesson introduces the masculine, singular demonstrative pronoun **ذَلِكَ** (dhâlika), meaning "that", used for objects or people that are far away. It also introduces the conjunction **وَ** (wa), meaning "and".

## 2. Grammar Notes & Points to Remember
*   **Pronunciation of ذَلِكَ:** It is pronounced *dhâlika* (with a long 'a' after the 'dh'), but it is written without the vertical *alif*.
*   **Usage of وَ (wa):** The Arabic word for "and" is a single letter (وَ). In written Arabic, it is attached close to the following word rather than floating as a separate word with wide spaces.
*   **Combining Sentences:** You can now connect sentences to contrast near and far objects. (e.g., *This* is a house, *and that* is a mosque).

## 3. Vocabulary List

| Arabic (with vowels) | Transliteration | English Translation | Category |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ذَلِكَ | dhâlika | that (masculine singular) | Pronoun |
| وَ | wa | and | Conjunction |
| إِمَامٌ | imâmu-n | an imam / prayer leader | Person |
| حَجَرٌ | ḥajaru-n | a stone | Object |
| سُكَّرٌ | sukkaru-n | sugar | Food |
| لَبَنٌ | labanu-n | milk | Food |

*(Note: This lesson heavily reuses vocabulary from Lesson 1 to practice the new concepts).*

## 4. Example Sentences & Dialogues

**Basic "That is..." Statements:**
*   **Q:** مَا ذَلِكَ؟ (What is that?)
*   **A:** ذَلِكَ نَجْمٌ. (That is a star.)
*   **Q:** مَا ذَلِكَ؟ (What is that?)
*   **A:** ذَلِكَ حَجَرٌ. (That is a stone.)

**Combining "This" and "That":**
*   هَذَا مَسْجِدٌ وَذَلِكَ بَيْتٌ. (This is a mosque and that is a house.)
*   هَذَا حِصَانٌ وَذَلِكَ حِمَارٌ. (This is a horse and that is a donkey.)
*   هَذَا سُكَّرٌ وَذَلِكَ لَبَنٌ. (This is sugar and that is milk.)

**Who is this and who is that?**
*   **Q:** مَنْ هَذَا وَمَنْ ذَلِكَ؟ (Who is this and who is that?)
*   **A:** هَذَا مُدَرِّسٌ وَذَلِكَ إِمَامٌ. (This is a teacher and that is an imam.)

**Yes/No Questions with "That":**
*   **Q:** أَذَلِكَ كَلْبٌ؟ (Is that a dog?)
*   **A:** لا، ذَلِكَ قِطٌّ. (No, that is a cat.)
# Lesson 3 (الدَّرْسُ الثَّالِثُ)

## 1. Core Concepts: The Definite Article "The" (ال) and Simple Sentences
This lesson introduces how to make a noun definite by adding the Arabic equivalent of "the" (**ال** / *al-*). It also teaches how to construct simple nominal sentences using a definite subject and an indefinite adjective (e.g., "The door is open").

## 2. Grammar Notes & Points to Remember
*   **The Definite Article and Tanwīn:** 
    *   In Arabic, a noun cannot have both the definite article (*al-*) and the indefinite marker (*tanwīn* / the "-n" sound) at the same time. 
    *   When **ال** (*al-*) is prefixed to a noun, the double vowel (*tanwīn*) at the end drops to a single vowel.
    *   *Example:* بَيْتٌ (*baitu-n* / a house) becomes الْبَيْتُ (*al-baitu* / the house).
*   **Moon Letters (الْحُرُوفُ الْقَمَرِيَّةُ):** 
    *   There are 14 Moon letters. When *al-* is attached to a word starting with a Moon letter, the "l" (ل) is pronounced clearly. 
    *   *Example:* اَلْقَمَرُ (*al-qamaru* / the moon).
*   **Sun Letters (الْحُرُوفُ الشَّمْسِيَّةُ):** 
    *   There are 14 Sun letters (e.g., t, d, r, s, sh, n). When *al-* is attached to a word starting with a Sun letter, the "l" is assimilated (swallowed) into the Sun letter. This is marked by a *shaddah* ( ّ ) on the Sun letter.
    *   *Example:* اَلشَّمْسُ (*ash-shamsu* / the sun) — not *al-shamsu*.
    *   *Example:* اَلنَّجْمُ (*an-najmu* / the star) — not *al-najmu*.
*   **Hamzat al-Waṣl (هَمْزَةُ الْوَصْلِ):**
    *   The "a" sound in *al-* is only pronounced when it starts a sentence. If it is preceded by another word (like **وَ** / *wa* / and), the "a" is dropped in pronunciation (though still written).
    *   *Example:* الْبَيْتُ (al-baitu), but وَالْبَيْتُ (wa-l-baitu).
*   **Simple Nominal Sentences:** 
    *   To say "The pen is broken," the subject ("The pen") is definite (*al-qalamu*), and the predicate ("broken") remains indefinite with *tanwīn* (*maksūru-n*).
    *   *Example:* الْقَلَمُ مَكْسُورٌ (*al-qalamu maksūrun*).

# Lesson 4 (الدَّرْسُ الرَّابِعُ)

## 1. Core Concepts: Prepositions (حُرُوفُ الْجَرِّ) and Pronouns
This lesson introduces prepositions (words like "in", "on", "from", "to") and how they change the vowel ending of the noun that follows them[cite: 39]. It also introduces the singular third-person pronouns for "he/it" and "she/it"[cite: 39].

## 2. Grammar Notes & Points to Remember
*   **The Genitive Case (مَجْرُورٌ):** 
    *   The normal ending of an Arabic noun is the nominative case (*-u* or *-un*), e.g., اَلْبَيْتُ (*al-baitu*)[cite: 39].
    *   When a noun is preceded by a preposition, its ending changes to the genitive case (*-i* or *-in*), e.g., فِي الْبَيْتِ (*fī l-baiti* / in the house)[cite: 39].
*   **Third-Person Pronouns:** 
    *   **هُوَ (huwa):** Means "he" or "it" (for masculine nouns)[cite: 39].
    *   **هِيَ (hiya):** Means "she" or "it" (for feminine nouns)[cite: 40].
    *   Because all Arabic nouns are either masculine or feminine, these pronouns are used for people, animals, and inanimate objects alike[cite: 39, 40].
*   **Feminine Proper Nouns:** 
    *   Unlike masculine names (like مُحَمَّدٌ / *Muḥammadun*), feminine proper names do not take *tanwīn*. They end with a single vowel, e.g., آمِنَةُ (*Āminatu*), زَيْنَبُ (*Zainabu*), فَاطِمَةُ (*Fāṭimatu*)[cite: 35, 40].
*   **Basic Verbs:** 
    *   This lesson introduces past-tense verbs like ذَهَبَ (*dhahaba* / he went) and خَرَجَ (*kharaja* / he left)[cite: 38].

# Lesson 5 (الدَّرْسُ الْخَامِسُ)

## 1. Core Concepts: Possession (الإِضَافَةُ - Al-Idafah)
This lesson teaches how to show possession or relationship between two nouns, such as "Muhammad's book" or "the teacher's desk"[cite: 41, 47]. This structure is called *Idafah*.

## 2. Grammar Notes & Points to Remember
*   **Structure of Possession (*Idafah*):** 
    *   **The Possessed (*Mudāf* / مُضَافٌ):** Always comes first. It never takes the definite article (ال) and never takes *tanwīn*. E.g., كِتَابُ (*kitābu*)[cite: 47].
    *   **The Possessor (*Mudāf ilaihi* / مُضَافٌ إِلَيْهِ):** Always comes second. It is always in the genitive case (takes a *-i* or *-in* ending). E.g., مُحَمَّدٍ (*Muḥammadin*) or اَلْمُدَرِّسِ (*al-mudarrisi*)[cite: 47].
    *   *Example:* كِتَابُ بِلَالٍ (*kitābu Bilālin* / Bilal's book)[cite: 47].
    *   *Example:* بَيْتُ الْإِمَامِ (*baitu l-imāmi* / the Imam's house)[cite: 47].
*   **Asking "Whose?":** 
    *   The phrase لِمَنْ (*liman*) means "whose" or "belonging to whom"[cite: 47, 51]. It is indeclinable (does not change its vowel ending)[cite: 47].
*   **The Vocative Particle (يَا):** 
    *   When calling someone, use يَا (*yā* / O). The noun immediately following it loses its *tanwīn* and takes only a single *dammah* (*-u*). 
    *   *Example:* يَا بِلَالُ (*yā Bilālu*), not *yā Bilālun*[cite: 47, 48].
*   **Spatial Nouns:** 
    *   The word تَحْتَ (*taḥta* / under) acts like a possessed noun (*mudāf*). The noun following it becomes genitive (*mudāf ilaihi*). 
    *   *Example:* تَحْتَ الْمَكْتَبِ (*taḥta l-maktabi* / under the desk)[cite: 47].
*   **Hamzatu l-Waṣl in Nouns:** 
    *   The words اِسْمٌ (*ismun* / name) and اِبْنٌ (*ibnun* / son) begin with a *hamzatu l-waṣl*. When preceded by another word, the initial "i" sound is dropped in pronunciation[cite: 48]. 
    *   *Example:* اِسْمُ الْوَلَدِ بِلَالٌ (*ismu l-waladi Bilālun* / The boy's name is Bilal)[cite: 48].

# Lesson 6 (الدَّرْسُ السَّادِسُ)

## 1. Core Concepts: Feminine Nouns and "This" (هَذِهِ)
This lesson introduces the feminine demonstrative pronoun **هَذِهِ** (*hādhihi* / this), the rules for identifying feminine words in Arabic, and the preposition **لِـ** (*li* / belongs to).

## 2. Grammar Notes & Points to Remember
*   **The Feminine "This" (هَذِهِ):** 
    *   هَذِهِ is the feminine counterpart to هَذَا[cite: 54]. It is pronounced *hādhihi* with a long "a", though the vertical *alif* is omitted in writing[cite: 54].
    *   *Example:* هَذَا وَلَدٌ وَهَذِهِ بِنْتٌ. (This is a boy and this is a girl)[cite: 54].
*   **Feminine Markers (ة):** 
    *   Most nouns and adjectives are made feminine by adding a round *tā’* (ة / *tā’ marbūṭah*) to the end. The letter before it always takes a *fathah* (-a)[cite: 54].
    *   *Example:* مُدَرِّسٌ (*mudarrisun* / male teacher) -> مُدَرِّسَةٌ (*mudarrisatun* / female teacher)[cite: 54].
*   **Body Parts and Gender:** 
    *   In Arabic, paired body parts (hands, legs, eyes, ears) are generally treated as feminine[cite: 54].
    *   Single body parts (head, nose, mouth) are generally masculine[cite: 54].
*   **Preposition "Li" (لِـ):** 
    *   Meaning "belongs to" or "for". The noun following it becomes genitive (*-i* ending)[cite: 55].
    *   When combined with *man* (who), it becomes لِمَنْ (*liman* / whose / belonging to whom)[cite: 55].
    *   When attached to the word اللَّهُ (Allah), the *alif* is dropped entirely: لِلَّهِ (*lillāhi* / belongs to Allah)[cite: 55].

# Lesson 8 (الدَّرْسُ الثَّامِنُ)

## 1. Core Concepts: Demonstrative Phrases vs. Sentences
This lesson explains the critical difference between saying "This is a book" and "This book..."[cite: 64]. It also introduces invariable nouns (words that do not change their case endings) and adverbs of location[cite: 65].

## 2. Grammar Notes & Points to Remember
*   **Demonstrative Phrases (Definite Nouns after Demonstratives):**
    *   If a demonstrative pronoun (like هَذَا) is followed by an *indefinite* noun, it forms a complete sentence: هَذَا كِتَابٌ (*hādha kitābun* / This is a book)[cite: 64].
    *   If followed by a *definite* noun (with ال), it forms a phrase fragment: هَذَا الْكِتَابُ (*hādha l-kitābu* / This book...)[cite: 64]. To make it a complete sentence, you must add a predicate: هَذَا الْكِتَابُ جَدِيدٌ (*hādha l-kitābu jadīdun* / This book is new)[cite: 64].
*   **Invariable Nouns:** 
    *   Nouns ending in a long "ā" sound (usually written with an *alif* or *alif maqṣūrah* like ى) do not show grammatical case endings[cite: 65]. 
    *   They remain visually unchanged even after a preposition. Example: أَمْرِيكَا (America) remains أَمْرِيكَا whether it is the subject (أَمْرِيكَا) or follows a preposition (مِنْ أَمْرِيكَا / from America)[cite: 65].
*   **Adverbs of Place:** 
    *   Words like أَمَامَ (*amāma* / in front of) and خَلْفَ (*khalfa* / behind) act like possessed nouns (*muḍāf*)[cite: 65]. The noun that follows them takes the genitive case (*-i* ending)[cite: 65].

# Lesson 9 (الدَّرْسُ التَّاسِعُ)

## 1. Core Concepts: Adjectives (النَّعْتُ) and the Relative Pronoun (الَّذِي)
This lesson is divided into two parts. Part A introduces Arabic adjectives, which always follow the noun they describe and match them in specific ways[cite: 73]. Part B introduces the relative pronoun for "who" or "which"[cite: 74].

## 2. Grammar Notes & Points to Remember
*   **The Adjective (*Na't* / نَعْت) and the Noun (*Man'ūt* / مَنْعُوت):**
    *   In Arabic, the adjective comes *after* the noun it qualifies[cite: 73].
    *   **Agreement:** The adjective must perfectly match the noun in three ways[cite: 73]:
        1.  **Gender:** Masculine or Feminine (e.g., وَلَدٌ صَغِيرٌ / small boy; بِنْتٌ صَغِيرَةٌ / small girl)[cite: 73].
        2.  **Definiteness:** If the noun has ال, the adjective must have ال. If the noun has *tanwīn*, the adjective must have *tanwīn* (e.g., بَيْتٌ جَدِيدٌ / a new house; اَلْبَيْتُ الْجَدِيدُ / the new house)[cite: 73].
        3.  **Case Ending:** Nominative, Accusative, or Genitive (e.g., فِي بَيْتٍ جَدِيدٍ / in a new house)[cite: 73].
*   **Adjectives Ending in -ān:** 
    *   Adjectives that describe temporary states (like hungry, thirsty, angry) often end in *-ān* (ان). These words *never* take *tanwīn* (e.g., جَوْعَانُ / *jaw'ānu*, not *jaw'ānun*)[cite: 74].
*   **The Relative Pronoun (الَّذِي):** 
    *   الَّذِي (*alladhī*) means "who" (for humans) or "which/that" (for objects)[cite: 74]. It is used for masculine, singular nouns[cite: 74].
*   **Preposition "Inda" (عِنْدَ):** 
    *   Means "with" (in the sense of location, like "at someone's place" or "in someone's presence"). The noun following it is genitive[cite: 75].

# Lesson 10 (الدَّرْسُ الْعَاشِرُ)

## 1. Core Concepts: Attached Possessive Pronouns and Accompaniment (مَعَ)
This lesson shifts from showing possession between two separate nouns (Idafah) to using attached pronoun suffixes (my, your, his, her)[cite: 82]. It also explains how to say you are "with" someone and the difference between expressing ownership of relationships versus physical items[cite: 83, 84].

## 2. Grammar Notes & Points to Remember
*   **Attached Possessive Pronouns:** 
    These suffixes are attached directly to the end of a noun[cite: 82]:
    *   **ـكَ (-ka):** your (masculine singular). E.g., كِتَابُكَ (*kitābuka* / your book)[cite: 82].
    *   **ـهُ (-hu):** his. E.g., كِتَابُهُ (*kitābuhu* / his book)[cite: 82].
    *   **ـهَا (-hā):** her. E.g., كِتَابُهَا (*kitābuhā* / her book)[cite: 82].
    *   **ـِي (-ī):** my. E.g., كِتَابِي (*kitābī* / my book)[cite: 82]. (Note: The vowel before the "yā" is forced into a *kasrah* to match the sound).
*   **Special Nouns (أَبٌ and أَخٌ):** 
    *   The words for "father" (*abun*) and "brother" (*akhun*) are irregular. When you attach a pronoun to them (except for "my"), an extra **و** (*waw*) is inserted[cite: 82, 83].
    *   *Example:* أَبُوكَ (*abūka* / your father), NOT *abuka*[cite: 82].
    *   *Example:* أَخُوهُ (*akhūhu* / his brother), NOT *akhuhu*[cite: 82].
    *   *Exception:* أَبِي (*abī* / my father) and أَخِي (*akhī* / my brother) do not take the extra *waw*[cite: 83].
*   **Possession: "Li" vs. "Inda":** 
    *   **لِـ (li / to belong to):** Used for human relationships or body parts (things that are inseparable). E.g., لِي أَخٌ (*lī akhun* / I have a brother)[cite: 83].
    *   **عِنْدَ ('inda / with):** Used for physical, separable possessions. E.g., عِنْدِي سَيَّارَةٌ (*'indī sayyāratun* / I have a car)[cite: 83].
*   **Accompaniment "Ma'a" (مَعَ):** 
    *   Means "with" (accompanied by)[cite: 84]. The noun following it takes the genitive case. E.g., مَعَ خَالِدٍ (*ma'a Khālidin* / with Khalid)[cite: 80]. It can also take pronoun suffixes: مَعِي (*ma'ī* / with me), مَعَكَ (*ma'aka* / with you)[cite: 81].
*   **Past Tense Subject Pronouns:** 
    *   ذَهَبَ (*dhahaba* / he went)[cite: 83].
    *   ذَهَبْتَ (*dhahabta* / you went - masculine singular)[cite: 83].
    *   ذَهَبْتُ (*dhahabtu* / I went)[cite: 83].
*   **Masculine Names ending in ة:** 
    *   Names for men that end in a *tā' marbūṭah* (like حَمْزَةُ / Hamzatu, أُسَامَةُ / Usāmatu) do not take *tanwīn*[cite: 84].

## 4. Example Sentences & Dialogues

**Using Attached Pronouns:**
*   **Q:** مَا اسْمُكَ؟ (What is your name?)[cite: 76].
*   **A:** اسْمِي مُحَمَّدٌ. (My name is Muhammad.)[cite: 76].
*   **Q:** وَحَمْزَةُ، مَا لُغَتُهُ؟ (And Hamzah, what is his language?)[cite: 77].
*   **A:** لُغَتُهُ الْيَابَانِيَّةُ. (His language is Japanese.)[cite: 77].

**Special Words (Father and Brother):**
*   **Q:** أَيْنَ أَبُوكَ يَا مُحَمَّدُ؟ (Where is your father, O Muhammad?)[cite: 77].
*   **A:** أَبِي فِي الْكُوَيْتِ. (My father is in Kuwait.)[cite: 77].
*   وَزَمِيلُكَ، أَيْنَ أَبُوهُ؟ (And your classmate, where is his father?)[cite: 77].

**Possession (Relationships vs. Objects):**
*   أَلَكَ أَخٌ يَا مُحَمَّدُ؟ نَعَمْ لِي أَخٌ وَاحِدٌ. (Do you have a brother, O Muhammad? Yes, I have one brother.)[cite: 77].
*   أَعِنْدَكَ سَيَّارَةٌ يَا أَخِي؟ لَا، مَا عِنْدِي سَيَّارَةٌ. (Do you have a car, my brother? No, I do not have a car.)[cite: 77].

**Using "Ma'a" (With):**
*   وَمَنْ هَذَا الْفَتَى الَّذِي مَعَكَ؟ (And who is this young man who is with you?)[cite: 76].
*   خَرَجَ أَبِي مِنَ الْبَيْتِ. مَنْ خَرَجَ مَعَهُ؟ خَرَجَ مَعَهُ عَمِّي. (My father left the house. Who left with him? My uncle left with him.)[cite: 81].
