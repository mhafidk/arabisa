# Lesson 1 (الدَّرْسُ الأَوَّلُ)

## 1. Core Concept: The Demonstrative Pronoun "This" (هَذَا)

This lesson introduces the masculine, singular demonstrative pronoun **هَذَا** (hâdhâ), which translates to "this". It is used to point to objects or people that are close by.

## 2. Grammar Notes & Points to Remember

- **Pronunciation of هَذَا:** It is pronounced _hâdhâ_ (with a long 'a' after the 'h'), even though it is written without the first _alif_.
- **The "Is" Copula:** Arabic has no word corresponding exactly to the English "is" in these types of sentences. "This is a house" is simply "This (a) house" (هَذَا بَيْتٌ).
- **The Indefinite Article ("a" / "an"):** There is no separate word for "a" or "an". The "-n" sound at the end of an Arabic noun (e.g., _baitu-n_, _masjidu-n_) acts as the indefinite article. This "-n" sound is called **tanwīn** (تَنْوِين), represented by the double vowel marker (ٌ ).
- **Forming Questions:**
  - **What is...? (مَا):** Used for objects/animals. Example: مَا هَذَا؟ (What is this?)
  - **Who is...? (مَنْ):** Used for people/intellects. Example: مَنْ هَذَا؟ (Who is this?)
  - **Is this...? (أَ):** Placing the particle **أَ** (a) at the beginning of a statement turns it into a Yes/No question.
    - Example: أَهَذَا بَيْتٌ؟ (Is this a house?)

## 4. Example Sentences & Dialogues

**Basic Statements:**

- هَذَا بَيْتٌ. (This is a house.)
- هَذَا مَسْجِدٌ. (This is a mosque.)
- هَذَا مِفْتَاحٌ. (This is a key.)

**Asking "What is this?" (مَا هَذَا؟):**

- **Q:** مَا هَذَا؟ (What is this?)
- **A:** هَذَا قَمِيصٌ. (This is a shirt.)
- **Q:** مَا هَذَا؟ (What is this?)
- **A:** هَذَا نَجْمٌ. (This is a star.)

**Yes/No Questions (أَهَذَا...؟):**

- **Q:** أَهَذَا بَيْتٌ؟ (Is this a house?)
- **A:** نَعَمْ، هَذَا بَيْتٌ. (Yes, this is a house.)
- **Q:** أَهَذَا مِفْتَاحٌ؟ (Is this a key?)
- **A:** لا، هَذَا قَلَمٌ. (No, this is a pen.)
- **Q:** أَهَذَا سَرِيرٌ؟ (Is this a bed?)
- **A:** لا، هَذَا كُرْسِيٌّ. (No, this is a chair.)

**Asking "Who is this?" (مَنْ هَذَا؟):**

- **Q:** مَنْ هَذَا؟ (Who is this?)
- **A:** هَذَا طَبِيبٌ. (This is a doctor.)
- **Q:** مَنْ هَذَا؟ (Who is this?)
- **A:** هَذَا طَالِبٌ. (This is a student.)
- **Q:** أَهَذَا وَلَدٌ؟ (Is this a boy?)
- **A:** لا، هَذَا رَجُلٌ. (No, this is a man.)

# Lesson 2 (الدَّرْسُ الثَّانِي)

## 1. Core Concepts: "That" (ذَلِكَ) and "And" (وَ)

This lesson introduces the masculine, singular demonstrative pronoun **ذَلِكَ** (dhâlika), meaning "that", used for objects or people that are far away. It also introduces the conjunction **وَ** (wa), meaning "and".

## 2. Grammar Notes & Points to Remember

- **Pronunciation of ذَلِكَ:** It is pronounced _dhâlika_ (with a long 'a' after the 'dh'), but it is written without the vertical _alif_.
- **Usage of وَ (wa):** The Arabic word for "and" is a single letter (وَ). In written Arabic, it is attached close to the following word rather than floating as a separate word with wide spaces.
- **Combining Sentences:** You can now connect sentences to contrast near and far objects. (e.g., _This_ is a house, _and that_ is a mosque).

## 3. Vocabulary List

| Arabic (with vowels) | Transliteration | English Translation       | Category    |
| :------------------- | :-------------- | :------------------------ | :---------- |
| ذَلِكَ               | dhâlika         | that (masculine singular) | Pronoun     |
| وَ                   | wa              | and                       | Conjunction |
| إِمَامٌ              | imâmu-n         | an imam / prayer leader   | Person      |
| حَجَرٌ               | ḥajaru-n        | a stone                   | Object      |
| سُكَّرٌ              | sukkaru-n       | sugar                     | Food        |
| لَبَنٌ               | labanu-n        | milk                      | Food        |

_(Note: This lesson heavily reuses vocabulary from Lesson 1 to practice the new concepts)._

## 4. Example Sentences & Dialogues

**Basic "That is..." Statements:**

- **Q:** مَا ذَلِكَ؟ (What is that?)
- **A:** ذَلِكَ نَجْمٌ. (That is a star.)
- **Q:** مَا ذَلِكَ؟ (What is that?)
- **A:** ذَلِكَ حَجَرٌ. (That is a stone.)

**Combining "This" and "That":**

- هَذَا مَسْجِدٌ وَذَلِكَ بَيْتٌ. (This is a mosque and that is a house.)
- هَذَا حِصَانٌ وَذَلِكَ حِمَارٌ. (This is a horse and that is a donkey.)
- هَذَا سُكَّرٌ وَذَلِكَ لَبَنٌ. (This is sugar and that is milk.)

**Who is this and who is that?**

- **Q:** مَنْ هَذَا وَمَنْ ذَلِكَ؟ (Who is this and who is that?)
- **A:** هَذَا مُدَرِّسٌ وَذَلِكَ إِمَامٌ. (This is a teacher and that is an imam.)

**Yes/No Questions with "That":**

- **Q:** أَذَلِكَ كَلْبٌ؟ (Is that a dog?)
- **A:** لا، ذَلِكَ قِطٌّ. (No, that is a cat.)

# Lesson 3 (الدَّرْسُ الثَّالِثُ)

## 1. Core Concepts: The Definite Article "The" (ال) and Simple Sentences

This lesson introduces how to make a noun definite by adding the Arabic equivalent of "the" (**ال** / _al-_). It also teaches how to construct simple nominal sentences using a definite subject and an indefinite adjective (e.g., "The door is open").

## 2. Grammar Notes & Points to Remember

- **The Definite Article and Tanwīn:**
  - In Arabic, a noun cannot have both the definite article (_al-_) and the indefinite marker (_tanwīn_ / the "-n" sound) at the same time.
  - When **ال** (_al-_) is prefixed to a noun, the double vowel (_tanwīn_) at the end drops to a single vowel.
  - _Example:_ بَيْتٌ (_baitu-n_ / a house) becomes الْبَيْتُ (_al-baitu_ / the house).
- **Moon Letters (الْحُرُوفُ الْقَمَرِيَّةُ):**
  - There are 14 Moon letters. When _al-_ is attached to a word starting with a Moon letter, the "l" (ل) is pronounced clearly.
  - _Example:_ اَلْقَمَرُ (_al-qamaru_ / the moon).
- **Sun Letters (الْحُرُوفُ الشَّمْسِيَّةُ):**
  - There are 14 Sun letters (e.g., t, d, r, s, sh, n). When _al-_ is attached to a word starting with a Sun letter, the "l" is assimilated (swallowed) into the Sun letter. This is marked by a _shaddah_ ( ّ ) on the Sun letter.
  - _Example:_ اَلشَّمْسُ (_ash-shamsu_ / the sun) — not _al-shamsu_.
  - _Example:_ اَلنَّجْمُ (_an-najmu_ / the star) — not _al-najmu_.
- **Hamzat al-Waṣl (هَمْزَةُ الْوَصْلِ):**
  - The "a" sound in _al-_ is only pronounced when it starts a sentence. If it is preceded by another word (like **وَ** / _wa_ / and), the "a" is dropped in pronunciation (though still written).
  - _Example:_ الْبَيْتُ (al-baitu), but وَالْبَيْتُ (wa-l-baitu).
- **Simple Nominal Sentences:**
  - To say "The pen is broken," the subject ("The pen") is definite (_al-qalamu_), and the predicate ("broken") remains indefinite with _tanwīn_ (_maksūru-n_).
  - _Example:_ الْقَلَمُ مَكْسُورٌ (_al-qalamu maksūrun_).

## 4. Example Sentences & Dialogues

**Applying the Definite Article:**

- بَيْتٌ -> اَلْبَيْتُ (_baitu-n_ -> _al-baitu_ / a house -> the house)
- قَلَمٌ -> اَلْقَلَمُ (_qalamu-n_ -> _al-qalamu_ / a pen -> the pen)
- نَجْمٌ -> اَلنَّجْمُ (_najmu-n_ -> _an-najmu_ / a star -> the star - _Sun letter_)
- تَاجِرٌ -> اَلتَّاجِرُ (_tājiru-n_ -> _at-tājiru_ / a merchant -> the merchant - _Sun letter_)

**Simple Descriptive Sentences:**

- اَلْقَلَمُ مَكْسُورٌ. (The pen is broken.)
- اَلْبَابُ مَفْتُوحٌ. (The door is open.)
- اَلْقَمَرُ جَمِيلٌ. (The moon is beautiful.)
- اَلْمَاءُ بَارِدٌ. (The water is cold.)
- اَللَّبَنُ حَارٌ. (The milk is hot.)
- اَلْقَمِيصُ نَظِيفٌ. (The shirt is clean.)
- اَلْحَجَرُ ثَقِيلٌ وَالْوَرَقُ خَفِيفٌ. (The stone is heavy and the paper is light.)

**Contrasting Subjects (Using "wa"):**

- اَلْكِتَابُ جَدِيدٌ وَالْقَلَمُ قَدِيمٌ. (The book is new and the pen is old.)
- اَلْحِمَارُ صَغِيرٌ وَالْحِصَانُ كَبِيرٌ. (The donkey is small and the horse is big.)
- اَلْوَلَدُ جَالِسٌ وَالْمُدَرِّسُ وَاقِفٌ. (The boy is sitting and the teacher is standing.)
- اَلْبَيْتُ قَرِيبٌ وَالْمَسْجِدُ بَعِيدٌ. (The house is near and the mosque is far away.)
- اَلطَّبِيبُ طَوِيلٌ وَالْمُدَرِّسُ قَصِيرٌ. (The doctor is tall and the teacher is short.)
